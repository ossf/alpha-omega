# 2026-09

## PyPI Safety & Security Engineer (Mike Fiedler)

### Malware & Abuse Response

151 malware reports arrived in September, covering 123 unique packages.
Reporting is concentrated: five reporters filed 63% of new reports,
and a single independent researcher filed 42% of them.
120 reports were confirmed malicious, seven were false alarms, and 24 are still under review.
Trusted observers were correct in all 78 of their reports,
and other users were correct 86% of the time.

The median malicious package was reported 2.1 hours after upload and acted on 2.4 hours after upload.
Auto-quarantine contained 67 packages (54.5%) as soon as reports arrived,
while full removal took a median of about 30 hours.
Half of September's reports were closed within 24 hours, with a median of about 22 hours.
All timings in this report are wall-clock hours, so they aren't directly comparable with August's working-hour figures.

Older reports wait much longer.
Mike closed 85 reports carried over from earlier months, some dating back to April.
Including those, the slowest fifth of September closures had waited more than five weeks.
A 28-package report from late July remains open.

Mike also published an advisory for an exposure in MemoryOS
([PYSEC-2026-3987](https://github.com/pypa/advisory-database/pull/390)).

### Account Safety & Email Reputation

Email reputation checks are now live on PyPI and TestPyPI.
Mike shipped the [email reputation service](https://github.com/pypi/warehouse/pull/20473)
and [wired it into email validation to reject disposable addresses](https://github.com/pypi/warehouse/pull/20506).
Since it was enabled mid-month, it has blocked 117 disposable email addresses
and permanently blocked 75 disposable domains,
with no support requests reporting a false positive.

### Python.org Content-Security-Policy

The python.org website does not have a `Content-Security-Policy`,
and so we're taking steps to add one to protect users
from injected scripts while they download Python artifacts.
We've added a [strict initial header value](https://github.com/python/pythondotorg/pull/3112) in `Report-Only` mode
and are monitoring from Sentry and [plan to address the findings](https://github.com/python/pythondotorg/pull/3113)
before switching over to an enforcing policy.

During this process we discovered a handful of URLs for
external resources that didn't explicitly require for HTTPS,
and so were upgraded to require HTTPS.

### Python Security Response Team

Prepared the patches, advisories, and CVEs for
five vulnerabilities affecting CPython from medium-critical severity:

* [CVE-2026-19445, CRITICAL] Use-after-free of a server-side SSLContext when sni_callback switches contexts 
* [CVE-2026-19553, HIGH] SSLContext.wrap_bio() missing validation of server_hostname parameter 
* [CVE-2026-82049, HIGH] tarfile extraction filters allow file modification and content disclosure via hard link to symlink 
* [CVE-2026-87910, MEDIUM] tarfile hardlink fallback ignores custom extraction filter rejection via None
* [CVE-2026-12345, MEDIUM] Race condition in tempfile.TemporaryDirectory cleanup allows deleting files outside the temporary directory 

Arranged for these patches to be backported and available
on older Python versions, and a security-fix release is
upcoming for the Python 3.14, 3.13, 3.12, 3.11, and 3.10 branches.

The PSRT is preparing for its first review of membership
since PEP 811 was accepted as its governance document. The
Steering Council has recommended shortening the evaluation
period for activity to 6 months instead of 12 months.
PSRT admins will be running a script and sharing the results
with the SC to determine which members to remove based on inactivity.
